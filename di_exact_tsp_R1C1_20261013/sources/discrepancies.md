## Meaning and limits of suitability

The manuscript provides a constrained preliminary entropic OT criterion, not an objective that jointly optimizes the final hard partition, medoids, and DI. Its phrase “equally or nearly suitable” supplies no numerical equivalence tolerance and refers to distinct sample-induced references, not proved invariance for a fixed sample.

We record J(P,O)=sum P*C_norm + .01 sum P(log P-1), with C_norm=||x-o||²/(median+1e-12), at each converged preliminary solution. Feasibility is checked on both marginals. Equal J and feasibility mean equal preliminary loss, not proved equal suitability after hardening. The cost median is recomputed by the manuscript procedure; thus different solutions use different scale factors. Native J is not evidence that a single fixed-scale global clustering objective was minimized. Raw transport cost and a common-scale loss (official seed0 median) expose this distinction.

For each final refined feasible soft system, Qsoft=sum pi_l*mu_li*||x_l-o_soft_i||² measures its spatial compactness in the same coordinates. Qhard uses weighted hard centroids; Qmedoid uses the actual medoid costs. These are diagnostics of the manuscript's compactness requirement; no additional optimization or selection by DI is performed.

We report all pairwise native-OT loss tolerances, and conservatively require both native OT and Qsoft closeness in a second analysis. Common-scale loss closeness is a further sensitivity check. This is an operational definition for the example, explicitly filling an unspecified comparison tolerance rather than pretending the manuscript supplies one. Two hard partitions with identical preliminary OT loss but different Qsoft are not simply declared interchangeable.

Each relative pair gap is |J_a-J_b|/max(|J_a|,|J_b|,1e-15) and |Qsoft_a-Qsoft_b|/min(Qsoft_a,Qsoft_b), respectively. Negative regularized losses are possible; absolute losses and gaps are always retained, and relative percentages depend on the specified entropy convention. Thresholds: numerical equality1e-10 relative,0.1%,1%,5%. No global optimality or statistical frequency claim is made from this finite set.
